Partial-ready decode candidate: actual-device rejection and diagnostic audit
2026-10-16; read-only analysis of root-collected S26 Ultra results.

Decision
The normal candidate is rejected. The three uninstrumented OFF/ON pairs all
slowed down: OFF 17.4577 / 17.6649 / 17.5824 TPS versus ON 17.1720 / 17.1169 /
17.1628 TPS. Medians are 17.5824 -> 17.1628 TPS (-2.3865%). Every run matched
all 65 output IDs. Root normal decision records maximum observed full-process
peak 2733428 -> 2733652 KiB (+224 KiB, sampled); no memory-growth explanation
or memory leak is inferred from this small difference. C16, preload128,
PAGE_CACHE=0, GROW=0 and existing reader counts were held fixed. Root restored
its production path. No additional partial-ready experiments are recommended.

Diagnostic validity
The first diagnostic used FARF(ALWAYS), which maps to SDK HAP_LEVEL_HIGH.
Its .log and .logcat.txt contain zero [LFM-PARTIAL] markers in BOTH runs.
The strict parser exits 2, records explicit missing-evidence errors and leaves
trace aggregates null. They are not treated as evidence of zero early work.
Original sources, binary evidence and result files remain preserved.

The logging-only v2 diagnostic uses FARF(ERROR). Its v81 TU compiles with
-O3 -Wall -Werror and NNTR_LFM2_PARTIAL_TRACE=1. The complete diagnostic patch
is relative to the frozen production candidate, not relative to diagnostic v1.
Both v2 .logcat.txt files contain exactly 64 ordered tokens 0..63, all rc=0.
Neither app .log mirrors the DSP trace, which is accepted explicitly. There
are no malformed, duplicate, missing or stale token records. Both sets of
65 IDs are equal, their SHA256 matches the runner metadata, and the runner
also reports reference parity. The metadata model hash and all environment
settings match except PARTIAL_READY and the generated-ID output path.

Partial-ready therefore did execute early DSP work. 1262/1291=97.754% of
eligible rounds began some work before the full answer, and 2250/4044=55.638%
of miss rows were computed early. But 2250/2219=1.014 rows/batch reflects
mostly single-expert calls; total MoE calls rose by 44.278%. Non-wait MoE
work increased by 914 us/token (+9.56%) and gross MoE by 1371 us/token.
Measured exposed miss wait also increased by 457.5 us/token in this pair.
Moving waits into ready callbacks must not be described as eliminating them:
24.951 ms ready wait + 6.128 ms final wait remains approximately 31.078 ms.
The trace/pool wait differences (-1.469/-2.609 us per token) are tiny separate
timer-read overheads, not missing multi-millisecond events.

The earlier HIGH-level diagnostic pair corroborates greater non-wait cost:
miss wait fell 415.7 us/token but non-wait MoE grew 943 us/token. Only the
normal repeated runs establish the performance decision. The two diagnostic
pairs show that splitting actual work adds enough non-wait overhead to erase
any overlap benefit under the measured schedule. Aggregate data cannot assign
all of that overhead uniquely to LUT preparation, GEMV, cache maintenance,
rebind, grouping or polling. Net MoE is an average from graph per-kind profile,
not a directly observed per-token kernel timing. No hardware maximum or
universal impossibility of I/O overlap is claimed.
